To my dear Teachers and Professors,

To Those Who Taught Me, and Those Who Walked Beside Me

TL;DR: You taught me far more than what could be found in books. From my school days at New Horizon High School and Jodhpur Park Boys' School to my years at IISER Kolkata, you became my teachers, mentors, guides, and, in many ways, my elder brothers and sisters. Through lessons, conversations, laughter, shared meals, stories, failures, and countless ordinary moments, you helped shape not only what I know, but who I have become.

Happy Teacher's Day. 🌸

There are those whom life appoints to teach us, and there are those whom life, in its quieter generosity, gives us as companions upon the road; and when I look back upon the years that carried me from the classrooms of New Horizon High School and Jodhpur Park Boys' School, through the halls and laboratories of IISER Kolkata, I find that I have been fortunate enough to receive both gifts in the same people. For many of you began as my teachers and, somewhere along the way, became something dearer and more enduring: the elder brothers and sisters whom I could look towards when the road ahead appeared uncertain, and whose experience seemed like a lantern held a little farther along the path.

You taught me, certainly, the things that books contain and examinations demand: equations, experiments, theories, methods, and the patient discipline of learning. Yet I have come to believe that some of the lessons which remain longest in the heart are those which arrive almost unnoticed, between one conversation and another, after class, in a laboratory, over lunch or dinner, amidst laughter and stories, or in those unhurried moments when a teacher and a student cease, for a little while, to be merely teacher and student and simply become two people sharing something of life.

I remember those moments perhaps more clearly than I remember many of the lessons themselves. The stories you shared about your own journeys, the mistakes you admitted, the advice offered almost in passing, the laughter over something entirely unrelated to academics, and the simple comfort of knowing that there was someone older and wiser whom I could approach, all became a kind of education of their own. They taught me that knowledge does not live only in books, but also in the lives of those who have walked before us.

At IISER Kolkata, that education took another form. You gave me the freedom to question, to explore, to fail, to begin again, and eventually to discover questions of my own. Much of what I do today, whether research, programming, building projects, organising, or pursuing some improbable idea simply because it refuses to leave my mind, grew from that freedom to explore and from the confidence you placed in me before I had fully learned to place it in myself.

But beyond all of this, you taught me how to navigate life.

There were moments when I did not know what I was doing, where I was going, or whether I was capable of getting there. Sometimes you gave me advice; sometimes a story; sometimes nothing more than a patient ear. You did not always solve the problem, and you did not need to. Sometimes it was enough to know that someone I respected had listened.

Perhaps that is what makes a teacher become family.

A younger brother does not always need an elder brother to carry him; sometimes he only needs to know that, when the road becomes difficult, there is someone he may turn towards.

And so, when I remember my school and institute years, I remember people more than chapters. I remember voices, faces, corridors, laboratories, shared meals, laughter, questions, mistakes, encouragement, and the innumerable little moments through which a person quietly grows.

The boy who entered those classrooms with more questions than answers eventually became the student who carried those questions into IISER Kolkata, and that student is now trying to research, build, code, create, and discover where those questions might lead.

Whatever I become, I know I shall never be entirely self-made.

Behind every question stands someone who encouraged curiosity.
Behind every difficult problem, someone who taught patience.
Behind every attempt to build something, someone who once said:

“Try.”

To my teachers from New Horizon High School, Jodhpur Park Boys' School, and IISER Kolkata, thank you not only for teaching me, but for accompanying me; for sharing pieces of your lives with me, for laughing with me, guiding me, challenging me, and believing in me through the many versions of myself that have come and gone.

Wherever the road takes me next, I shall carry something of you with me.

And perhaps, one day, when another young student comes to me with an impossible question or an unfinished idea, I shall remember how you treated me.

I shall listen.

I shall smile.

And I shall say:

“Try.”

With love, respect, and endless gratitude,

Shuvam
